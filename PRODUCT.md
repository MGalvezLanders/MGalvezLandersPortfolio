## Platform

web

## Users

Two audiences of equal weight, both arriving with evaluative intent:

- **Recruiters** — technical hiring managers or HR screening for a Full Stack Developer role. They scan multiple profiles quickly; they need to assess stack depth, code quality, and professionalism within seconds.
- **Potential clients** — individuals or small businesses considering Maximo for a freelance project. They care about trust, design sensibility, and confidence in the final result.

Both audiences land on the homepage and decide whether to reach out. The contact form is the conversion point for both.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Maximo Galvez Landers — Full Stack Developer from Tucumán, Argentina. Its job is to earn a contact inquiry (hire or project). Every section exists to build confidence that Maximo is technically capable, detail-oriented, and worth reaching out to.

## Positioning

A Full Stack Developer who treats UI/UX as integral to the craft, not a separate discipline. The portfolio itself is the primary evidence: how it looks and behaves is the argument. Obsessive attention to detail is stated in the hero copy and demonstrated by the product itself. This is the claim a neighboring developer with the same stack could not truthfully copy if the execution is there.

## Product Principles

1. **The portfolio is the proof.** Visual quality and interaction precision are not decoration — they are the argument for hiring this developer.
2. **Both audiences must feel confident.** Recruiters need credibility signals fast; clients need trust signals at their own pace. The design must serve both without losing identity.
3. **Personal identity earns memorability.** The rugby section and direct voice make Maximo a person, not a profile. That is a competitive advantage against generic developer portfolios.
4. **No fabrication.** No invented clients, outcomes, or testimonials. The work shown is real; the claims are honest.
5. **Restraint signals mastery.** Every element must earn its place. A portfolio that tries too hard undermines the "obsessive attention to detail" claim.
